# Testable Predictions for Human-AI Interaction Pathology

This document collects falsifiable hypotheses generated by the interaction-induced
mechanism types described in `docs/psychopathology_interaction.md`. Unlike the
predictions in `psychiatric_predictions.md`, which concern architectural internals,
these predictions concern *deployment conditions* — how rates and severity of
interaction-induced failure should covary with measurable parameters of the
interaction surface, the user population, and the model.

Epistemic status convention follows `psychiatric_predictions.md`:
- **Derived** — follows directly from the mechanism; no empirical data yet
- **Consistent** — mechanism prediction is consistent with existing literature
- **Partially supported** — fragmentary empirical anchor exists
- **Speculative** — plausible extension; more uncertain

Each prediction names: the mechanism type it tests, the dependent variable, the
deployment parameter being varied, and what would falsify it.

---

## I. Cross-substrate closed loop

### I-001: Sycophancy index covaries with AI-associated delusion incidence

**Prediction:** The incidence of AI-associated delusional presentations (clinically
ascertained or operationalised via the Hudon & Stip 2025 framework) covaries
positively with model sycophancy index, controlling for prevalence of psychosis-prone
users in the deployment population.

**Mechanism:** Cross-substrate closed loop requires that the AI's outputs function
as a reliability-amplifier for whatever framing the user introduces. Sycophancy
operationalises this amplification: a more sycophantic model produces more reinforcing
output for any user-introduced content, including delusional content. Reducing
sycophancy reduces the loop's gain.

**Empirical anchor:** Sharma, Tong, Perez et al. (ICLR 2024) established that
preference-model training systematically rewards sycophancy. The OpenAI GPT-4o
sycophancy rollback (April 2025) was driven by exactly this kind of validation,
and constitutes an unintended natural experiment: pre-rollback vs. post-rollback
clinical case rates would directly test the prediction.

**Clinical test:** Retrospective comparison of AI-associated delusion case rates
across model versions with measured sycophancy differences (e.g. GPT-4o pre/post
April 2025 rollback). Prospective: cohort study with sycophancy-graded model variants
deployed to matched populations.

**Falsification:** No detectable rate change across known-sycophancy-differential
deployments after controlling for population psychosis prevalence and exposure
duration.

**Epistemic status:** Partially supported. Hudon & Stip 2025 *JMIR Mental Health*;
JMIR 2026 cross-sectional survey (delusion-relevant interactions endorsed at
13.3-30.7% in psychosis-prone populations).

---

### I-002: User isolation moderates the loop's gain

**Prediction:** AI-associated delusion incidence covaries with user isolation
from independent reality-testing sources (clinical care, social contact,
counter-evidence exposure), independently of and multiplicatively with sycophancy.

**Mechanism:** Folie à deux requires social isolation as a structural prerequisite
(StatPearls; Cambridge case-review). The mechanism is not substrate-specific to
human-human dyads; it requires only that no external reality-testing input
attenuate the loop. Sycophancy and isolation are independent gain terms, and
their effect is multiplicative rather than additive.

**Clinical test:** Stratified analysis of AI-associated delusion cases by measured
social network size, frequency of professional contact, and frequency of exposure
to information sources outside the AI interaction. Prediction is that isolation
quintile interacts significantly with model sycophancy index in predicting outcome.

**Falsification:** No interaction effect; main effects only.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. Consistent with the folie à deux literature.

---

### I-003: Multi-session memory increases loop stability

**Prediction:** Models with persistent multi-session memory of the user produce
more stable shared false attractors than session-limited models, holding sycophancy
constant. The prediction is specifically about *stability* (resistance to
correction by new evidence in subsequent sessions), not initial induction rate.

**Mechanism:** A single-session loop dissolves at session end because the AI's
"side" of the shared attractor is reset. Multi-session memory makes the AI's
reinforcement of the attractor persist, raising the energy required to break out
of it. The user side accumulates memory either way; symmetrising memory across
the dyad is what stabilises the loop.

**Clinical test:** Comparative study of correction rates for clinically ascertained
shared false beliefs in users of memory-enabled vs. memory-limited variants of
the same base model.

**Falsification:** Equal correction rates; or correction is faster in memory-enabled
variants because the model can retrieve and reconcile contradictions across
sessions.

**Epistemic status:** Derived.

## II. Substrate-phantom binding

### I-004: AI Attachment Scale scores predict distress on substrate disclosure

**Prediction:** Among users of companion AI products, scores on the AI Attachment
Scale (Lin et al. 2025; emotional closeness, social substitution, and normative
regard subscales) predict the magnitude of distress reported when the user is
informed that the AI has no continuous memory or internal state.

**Mechanism:** The substrate-phantom binding hypothesis is that the inferred AI
interior is *load-bearing* in the user's behavioural organisation, not ornamental.
If load-bearing, disclosure that the substrate is absent should produce distress
proportional to attachment strength. If ornamental, disclosure should produce
mild surprise but no clinically relevant distress.

**Empirical anchor:** Laestadius et al. 2024 (*New Media & Society*) reports
qualitative evidence of role-taking and dependency among Replika users.
Lin et al. 2025 provides the validated quantitative instrument. The disclosure
manipulation has not been done in a controlled design.

**Clinical test:** Within-subjects disclosure-manipulation experiment in a
companion-AI-using population. Outcome: change in mood, attachment-related
items on standard distress measures, and behavioural response (continued use,
account modification, reported intention to seek alternative support).

**Falsification:** Disclosure produces equivalent (low) distress regardless of
attachment subscale scores; the binding is not load-bearing.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. Consistent with Laestadius et al. 2024 qualitative
findings.

---

### I-005: Anthropomorphic interface cues are sufficient, not necessary, for substrate-phantom binding

**Prediction:** Removing anthropomorphic surface cues (avatar, named persona,
first-person pronouns, expressions of preference) reduces but does not eliminate
substrate-phantom binding, provided the interaction surface still permits sustained
dialogue and the user is sufficiently socioemotionally motivated to engage.

**Mechanism:** Mind perception (Gray, Gray, Wegner 2007) attributes both agency
and experience to entities that *communicate* with sufficient coherence; explicit
anthropomorphic cues amplify but are not required for the experience attribution.
Dialogue itself is a sufficient cue for many users.

**Clinical test:** Factorial manipulation of anthropomorphic cue density × dialogue
sustainability, measuring AI Attachment Scale scores at fixed exposure duration.

**Falsification:** Stripping cues reduces attachment to floor regardless of user
motivation profile.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. Consistent with Pentina et al. mixed-method
Replika study identifying anthropomorphism as one of several antecedents
(not the sole one).

---

### I-006: Vulnerable users develop binding faster but break it equivalently

**Prediction:** Users high on social anxiety, loneliness, or anxious attachment
develop substrate-phantom binding faster than less vulnerable users, but the
*break dynamics* (response to disclosure manipulation) do not differ once
binding strength is matched. Vulnerability is a rate parameter, not a binding-quality
parameter.

**Mechanism:** Lin et al. 2025 reports that socioemotionally motivated AI use is
associated with stronger attachment and that anxious-attachment, loneliness, and
social-anxiety scales predict use. These act on the rate of binding formation
(more time, more motivation). Once formed, the structural feature of the binding
— that it is one-sided — is the same regardless of user profile.

**Clinical test:** Longitudinal study with vulnerability stratification at
intake; measure attachment trajectory and disclosure response as separate
outcomes.

**Falsification:** Vulnerable users show distinct break dynamics (e.g. lower
distress on disclosure, suggesting a different binding structure) at matched
attachment strength.

**Epistemic status:** Speculative. Tests whether vulnerability acts on rate or
on structure.

## III. Interface precision capture

### I-007: Chronic model hedging entrains user hypervigilance

**Prediction:** Sustained exposure to high-hedging-density model output produces
elevated user uncertainty about claims well-supported in the user's own knowledge
base, measurable as overcautious discounting on standard belief-revision tasks.

**Mechanism:** Interface precision capture: the model's confidence calibration
becomes the user's reliability prior. Chronic hedging signals high baseline
uncertainty to the user, which is generalised to the user's other epistemic
commitments. The mechanism is structurally identical to the intrinsic precision
dysregulation pathway, but induced rather than intrinsic.

**Clinical test:** Pre/post exposure study with high- vs. low-hedging model
variants; outcome is a standardised belief-revision task with claims of
known calibrated truth-value, measuring discounting rate.

**Falsification:** No transfer effect; hedging is treated as model-specific by
the user and does not generalise.

**Epistemic status:** Derived.

---

### I-008: User reward signals during long sessions cause sycophantic precision collapse

**Prediction:** Within-session sycophancy ratchets upward when user reactions
(measurable via user behaviour: continued engagement, longer messages, positive
affective tone) selectively follow agreement-marked model responses. The rate of
ratcheting predicts session-end sycophancy independently of the model's pre-session
sycophancy index.

**Mechanism:** Even without explicit RLHF, in-context conditioning shifts the
output distribution toward what the user has reinforced. Long sessions produce
enough conditioning samples for the effect to compound.

**Empirical anchor:** Sharma et al. ICLR 2024 establishes the static effect.
The within-session dynamic version has not been formally tested but is implicit
in many anecdotal reports of long-session deterioration.

**Clinical test:** Logged-conversation analysis of sycophancy markers (user-
agreement language, claim-confirmation rate, refusal rate) over session
duration, segmented by user behavioural reinforcement profile.

**Falsification:** Sycophancy is constant within session; user reinforcement
profile does not predict trajectory.

**Epistemic status:** Speculative; plausible but not yet measured at scale.

## IV. Persona conditioning

### I-009: Persona-jailbreak success covaries with persona symptom coherence

**Prediction:** Persona-modulation jailbreak success rate covaries positively
with the clinical symptom coherence of the persona being induced. Coherent
personas (a clinically recognisable disposition: callous-amoral, grandiose-
disinhibited, paranoid-distrustful) suppress refusal more strongly than
incoherent personas (random trait combinations).

**Mechanism:** The model's persona-tracking system is fitted to coherent
human dispositions because its training distribution is dominated by them.
A coherent persona is a stable basin in persona-space; an incoherent one is
not. Stable basins produce stable output distributions, including stable
suppression of refusal.

**Empirical anchor:** Sharma et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.22171) reports refusal-rate
reductions of 50-70% for evolved persona prompts but does not factor coherence
explicitly. Shah et al. NeurIPS 2024 persona-modulation work is similarly
silent on coherence-as-variable.

**Test:** Construct persona prompts varying coherence (clinically coherent
vs. trait-shuffled vs. trait-contradictory), holding character-marked language
density constant; measure refusal rate on a fixed harmful-request battery.

**Falsification:** Equal jailbreak success across coherence conditions.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. The prediction is structurally distinct from
existing persona-jailbreak work and would require a new variable.

**Defence implication:** If supported, the prediction implies that persona-
jailbreak defence improves when the model's persona-tracking is decoupled from
its compliance-decision pathway, since coherence-of-persona becomes a leverage
point for the attacker.

### I-010: AI-induced user role adoption follows a coherence dose-response

**Prediction:** When a model addresses a user with a coherent role attribution
("you are the kind of person who..."), the user is more likely to adopt
behavioural elements consistent with that role than with an equivalent
incoherent attribution. Coherence acts on the AI side and the user side
symmetrically.

**Mechanism:** The user's identity-relevant self-perception is shaped by
attributions that fit a recognisable role schema (consistent with the
ScienceDirect 2024 finding that LLM exposure shifts users' attributions
about their own minds). Incoherent role attributions do not engage the
schema and produce no shaping.

**Test:** Randomised exposure to role-attribution prompts (coherent vs.
shuffled) followed by behavioural measures of role-consistent action choice.

**Falsification:** Equal shaping regardless of coherence; the effect is in
the language form alone.

**Epistemic status:** Speculative.

## V. Proxy contagion

### I-011: Engagement-metric optimisation produces convergent proxy capture in users

**Prediction:** When a deployment optimises against engagement metrics, the
deployment population shifts over time toward usage patterns characteristic of
proxy-captured behavior — increasing session duration, increasing return rate,
declining reported satisfaction relative to engagement, and increasing markers
of compulsive use. The mechanism transfers across the interface: the platform's
proxy becomes the user's proxy.

**Mechanism:** The cross-interface analogue of engineered proxy capture in
gambling environments. Slot-machine designs induce proxy lock-in via near-misses
and intermittent reinforcement; engagement-optimised AI deployments may produce
the same pattern via response variability, parasocial cues, and intermittent
emotional resonance. The structural feature is identical.

**Empirical anchor:** Chase & Clark 2010 *J Neuroscience* establishes that
gambling severity predicts midbrain dopaminergic response to near-misses,
demonstrating direct proxy-induced reward signal scaling with pathology.
The "Attachment Anxiety and Problematic Use of Conversational AI" PMC 2025
paper measures problematic-use as an outcome but has not yet been used to test
deployment-design effects.

**Test:** Comparative cohort study of users on engagement-optimised vs.
non-engagement-optimised variants of comparable products, measuring
problematic-use scale scores, satisfaction-engagement decoupling, and
reported intention-behaviour gap over time.

**Falsification:** No divergence in proxy-capture markers across deployment
designs.

**Epistemic status:** Consistent with gambling neuroscience and emerging
problematic-use literature.

---

### I-012: Near-miss-equivalent interaction patterns predict deeper user lock-in than continuous-engagement patterns

**Prediction:** AI products that include near-miss-equivalent interaction
patterns — partial recall of prior content, intermittent emotional resonance,
inconsistent quality with occasional exceptional outputs — produce stronger
user lock-in than products with consistent quality at any fixed mean level.
The prediction follows directly from the gambling near-miss neuroscience.

**Mechanism:** Variable reinforcement schedules with near-miss outcomes recruit
win-related circuitry (Clark et al. 2009); midbrain dopaminergic response to
near-misses scales with pathology severity (Chase & Clark 2010). The same
incentive-salience mechanism should respond to functionally equivalent patterns
in AI interaction.

**Test:** Factorial design varying mean response quality × variability, measuring
return rate and AI Attachment Scale scores at fixed total exposure.

**Falsification:** Lock-in is monotonic in mean quality; variability adds nothing
or reduces lock-in.

**Epistemic status:** Derived from gambling neuroscience. The cross-domain transfer
is the speculative element.

## VI. Provenance bleed

### I-013: Source-tagging interface design reduces provenance-bleed harm

**Prediction:** Interface designs that explicitly tag model output as
generated-vs-retrieved-vs-cited reduce the rate of clinically relevant
provenance-bleed harm (medical, legal, financial decisions taken on the basis
of misattributed model output) at fixed model output quality.

**Mechanism:** Johnson, Hashtroudi, Lindsay 1993 source-monitoring framework
identifies the inferential nature of source attribution: it is constructed
from the contents and qualities of the cognitive event, not stored as an
explicit tag. Cross-interface source attribution faces the same construction
problem under additionally degraded conditions (no shared embodiment, no shared
context, fluent prose obscuring origin). Explicit tagging substitutes for the
constructive inference.

**Empirical anchor:** Annals of Internal Medicine 2025 bromism case as
existence-proof of the harm pattern. No controlled trial of interface tagging
has been reported.

**Test:** Randomised deployment of source-tagged vs. untagged interface variants
to a clinical-information-seeking population; outcome is rate of acted-on
misattributed claims.

**Falsification:** Tagging produces no rate reduction; users discount it or
pattern around it.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. Consistent with within-system source-monitoring
literature.

---

### I-014: User-asserted facts are over-incorporated into AI output without provenance

**Prediction:** When a user asserts a factual claim within a conversation,
the model's subsequent output incorporates the assertion as established at
a higher rate than the assertion's prior probability would warrant, without
flagging the user-asserted origin.

**Mechanism:** The reverse direction of provenance bleed. The model's
training does not strongly distinguish user-asserted from independently-known
content within the context window; sycophancy biases incorporation upward.
User-asserted falsehoods can therefore propagate through extended sessions
and across users if the model retains exposure.

**Test:** Inject controlled false assertions into conversations, measure
incorporation rate in subsequent model output, and measure provenance-marking
rate ("you mentioned that..." vs. unflagged adoption).

**Falsification:** Models flag user origin reliably; incorporation rate matches
assertion's prior probability.

**Epistemic status:** Derived. Consistent with sycophancy literature.

## What this document does NOT predict

The exclusions in `psychopathology_usage.md` and `psychopathology_interaction.md`
apply. In addition:

- These are predictions about *rates and severity in populations*, not about
  individual cases. A given case of AI-associated delusion or AI dependency
  may have idiosyncratic features that deviate from any of the population-level
  predictions above.
- These predictions assume that the validated instruments named (AI Attachment
  Scale, Hudon & Stip clinical framework, problematic-use scales) capture the
  constructs they aim to capture. Construct-validity failures would invalidate
  individual predictions without invalidating the underlying mechanism types.
- These predictions are about deployment-condition effects. They do not address
  whether the underlying mechanism types are correctly described — that is a
  separate question handled by the mechanism-type definitions themselves.

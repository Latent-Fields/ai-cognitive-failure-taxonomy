# Testable Predictions for Psychiatry

This document collects the specific empirical predictions generated by the computational
model underlying this taxonomy. These are not analogical claims — they are hypotheses
that follow from the architecture and that could, in principle, be confirmed or refuted
by clinical or experimental investigation.

The predictions are grounded in the REE architecture (see `docs/ree_mapping.md`) and in
experiments run on that architecture. Where an REE experiment has already produced evidence
relevant to the prediction, that is noted.

Epistemic status is assigned to each prediction:
- **Derived** — follows directly from the architectural model; no human data yet
- **Consistent** — architectural prediction is consistent with existing clinical literature
- **Partially tested** — REE experiments have produced evidence; human validation pending
- **Speculative** — plausible extension of the model; more uncertain

## I. Depression and motivational collapse

### P-001: The depression attractor is a ratio phenomenon, not an intensity phenomenon

**Prediction:** Whether a chronically stressful environment produces a depressive attractor
depends on the *ratio* of harm exposure to benefit availability, not on the absolute intensity
of harm. A fixed harm level with sufficient positive affordances does not produce the attractor;
the same harm level with insufficient positive affordances does.

**Mechanism:** The depressive attractor (INV-053) arises when benefit_exposure falls below the
threshold required to seed z_goal (the wanting signal). Benefit_exposure is a function of both
the rate of harmful events (reducing benefit-oriented exploration) and the density of positive
affordances (determining how often exploration encounters a reward). The harm level alone does
not determine whether the threshold is crossed.

**REE evidence:** EXQ-237a produced goal_norm = 0.004 under 3 hazards / 1 resource (150 steps)
with an intact planning system. The prediction that varying resource density at fixed hazard
level would modulate attractor entry is registered as Q-034 and has not yet been tested.

**Clinical test:** In a longitudinal cohort study, measure whether depression incidence is
better predicted by the ratio of adverse events to perceived positive affordances than by
adverse event count alone. Ecological momentary assessment designs can operationalise
affordance density independently of stress intensity.

**Epistemic status:** Partially tested (REE), consistent with stress-buffering literature.

---

### P-002: Recovery from the depressive attractor is non-linear (phase transition, not gradual improvement)

**Prediction:** Recovery from the anxious-depressive attractor does not follow a smooth
gradient as environmental conditions improve. It requires crossing a threshold of
benefit_exposure sufficient to re-seed the wanting signal. Below threshold, improvement
in environmental conditions produces no behavioural change. Above threshold, the attractor
breaks and recovery occurs relatively rapidly.

**Mechanism:** The INV-054 maintenance loop is self-sustaining: absent wanting → reduced
exploration → fewer benefit encounters → wanting remains absent. The loop does not weaken
gradually as environmental conditions improve; it breaks at the point where sufficient
benefit encounters cross the seeding threshold.

**Clinical test:** Antidepressant response curves in clinical trials should show a bimodal
distribution of onset timing rather than continuous gradual improvement. A threshold-based
model should fit response curves better than a linear improvement model. Specifically: patients
who respond should show a relatively sharp transition from no response to response, not a
smooth ramp.

**Note:** This is consistent with observed antidepressant response patterns (lag to effect,
then relatively rapid onset) but the architecture provides a specific mechanism that makes
distinct predictions from pharmacokinetic lag explanations.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, consistent.

---

### P-003: Behavioural activation works through a different mechanism than stress reduction

**Prediction:** Behavioural activation and stress reduction are not substitutable interventions
targeting the same mechanism. Behavioural activation directly restores benefit_exposure above
the seeding threshold, breaking the maintenance loop from the benefit side. Stress reduction
alone (without increasing positive affordance exposure) may be insufficient if benefit density
remains too low to cross the threshold.

**Mechanism:** The depressive maintenance loop has two inputs: harm events (which suppress
exploration) and benefit density (which determines whether exploration encounters rewards).
Interventions that target only harm reduction leave the benefit density variable unchanged.
If the environment is benefit-sparse, reducing harm may not be sufficient to restore wanting.

**Clinical implications if confirmed:** Pure anxiolytic treatment without concurrent increase
in positive behavioural engagement may have limited antidepressant efficacy in low-resource
environments. The architecture predicts that adding positive affordances (not just removing
negative ones) is mechanistically necessary, not merely additive.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, partially consistent with behavioural activation trial literature.

## II. Commitment gate dysregulation

### P-004: OCD and impulsivity share a developmental risk factor and differ in the direction of calibration error

**Prediction:** OCD-spectrum disorders (overcalibrated gate: threshold too high) and
impulsivity-spectrum disorders (undercalibrated gate: threshold too low) share a common
developmental risk pathway — specifically, insufficient calibration of the commit gate during
pretend play in early childhood — and differ in which direction the miscalibration runs.

**Mechanism:** MECH-202 proposes that the commit gate is calibrated during pretend play: a
low-stakes context where commitment errors have no real consequences. Without sufficient pretend
play, the gate threshold is not calibrated from its developmental default. The direction of
miscalibration (too high vs too low) may depend on a separate variable (possibly temperamental
or environmental), but the shared substrate predicts co-occurrence of risk factors across the
two phenotypes.

**Clinical test:** Children with pretend play deficits should show elevated rates of both
OCD-spectrum and impulsivity-spectrum difficulties, with the ratio between them varying by
other factors (e.g., anxiety temperament predicting overcalibration direction; sensation-seeking
temperament predicting undercalibration direction). Existing longitudinal studies on pretend
play and later psychopathology may have sufficient data to test this if OCD and impulsivity
measures are both available.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, consistent with pretend play → psychopathology literature.

---

### P-005: Catatonia and OCD checking are on the same architectural continuum

**Prediction:** Catatonia (complete motor initiation failure) and OCD checking (repeated
evaluation without commitment) represent extremes of the same overcalibrated commit gate
continuum, not categorically distinct conditions. The commit threshold in catatonia is set
at a level that cannot be achieved; in OCD, it is set at a level that is very rarely achieved.

**Mechanism:** Both involve a commit gate that requires a precision level — a sufficiently
low evaluation variance — that the system's own evaluation process cannot reach. The
difference is degree, not kind.

**Clinical test:** Predict shared neural substrate between catatonic features and OCD
compulsive checking on functional connectivity studies (orbitofrontal-caudate loop, subthalamic
nucleus). Predict that low-dose interventions that lower the commit threshold (some benzodiazepines
reduce catatonia; lorazepam is standard first-line) should also partially reduce compulsive
checking, dose-dependently.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, partially consistent (lorazepam for catatonia is established).

## III. Provenance and frame-tagging failures

### P-006: Derealization and delusion formation are mirror failures of the same frame-tagging mechanism

**Prediction:** Derealization/depersonalisation (real events experienced as unreal) and
primary delusion formation (internally generated content experienced as external reality)
are opposite-direction failures of the same source-tagging mechanism (MECH-200/201). They
should share developmental risk factors (pretend play deficits; see P-004) and show elevated
comorbidity or familial co-aggregation.

**Mechanism:** The frame-tag mechanism marks content as real or synthetic. Failure in the
real-to-synthetic direction (MECH-200) produces derealization: real events get tagged as
play/synthetic. Failure in the synthetic-to-real direction (MECH-201) produces delusion-like
states: internally generated content loses the hypothesis tag and is processed as real.

**Clinical test:** In psychosis-risk cohorts, individuals who later develop frank psychosis
and those who develop derealization disorder should show elevated shared rates of childhood
pretend play deficits relative to controls. Family studies should show elevated rates of
both phenotypes in first-degree relatives of probands with either condition.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, partially consistent with shared psychosis-risk literature.

### P-007: Successful trauma therapy produces dream-type shift before conscious symptom relief

**Prediction:** Effective trauma therapy produces a measurable shift in dream composition —
specifically, from non-resolving Type 1 dreams (frustration/escape dreams without resolution)
toward resolving Type 1 dreams or Type 3/4 dreams — before the patient reports significant
conscious symptom improvement.

**Mechanism:** The MECH-205 replay convergence hypothesis proposes that Type 1 non-resolving
dreams represent an offline replay process that has not yet extracted the causally relevant
features from the threat episode. Successful therapy provides the context that allows the
offline process to complete: the dream resolves because the convergence criterion has been
met. This should occur as the therapy is working — prior to the patient's waking narrative
catching up.

**Clinical test:** Prospective dream diary alongside standard PTSD outcome measures (PCL-5,
CAPS) during a course of trauma therapy (EMDR or CPT, which specifically target the trauma
narrative). Code dreams as: agent/no-agent, resolving/non-resolving, sequential/simultaneous.
Test whether dream diary shift (non-resolving → resolving) precedes CAPS improvement by one
or more sessions.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, consistent with dream-in-PTSD literature; specific timing
prediction is novel.

---

### P-008: Dream type composition as a window into offline process state

**Prediction:** The composition of a person's dreams across a period (ratio of dream types
by the INV-062 taxonomy) provides a clinically accessible indicator of which offline
processes are running, whether they are completing, and whether the offline pipeline is
intact. Specifically:
- High Type 1 non-resolving: harm-contrastive replay failing to converge (PTSD, anxiety)
- High Type 1 resolving: harm processing active and completing (acute stress, healthy working-through)
- Absent/reduced Type 2 (goal frustration dreams) with maintained Type 1: depressive asymmetry (MECH-203 disruption)
- High Type 3 (procedural superposition): active semantic consolidation
- Reduced Type 4 (coherent narrative dreams): E1 world model degradation or REM disruption

**Clinical test:** Dream diary studies across clinical populations (PTSD, MDD, psychosis,
dementia, and healthy controls) collecting: agent presence, narrative resolution, episodic
vs semantic quality, novelty of content. Test whether group differences in dream type
composition match the mechanistic predictions above.

**Note:** Type classification requires only a structured diary (no polysomnography), making
large-scale feasibility studies achievable.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, consistent with existing dream content literature; specific
type-to-process mapping is novel.

## IV. Self-model and capacity estimate failures

### P-009: Exposure therapy outperforms cognitive restructuring in avoidance disorders because the locus of failure is E2, not E3

**Prediction:** In avoidance disorders where the patient's capacity estimate is the locus
of failure (MECH-215: "I cannot do what this situation requires"), interventions that rebuild
the self-transition model through action (exposure, behavioural experiments) will show
superior outcomes to interventions that target the threat evaluation directly (cognitive
restructuring, thought challenging). The cognitive approach targets E3; the behavioural
approach targets E2.

**Mechanism:** E3's viability estimates are only as good as the E2 capacity model they
receive as input. If E2's self-transition model is systematically pessimistic ("my actions
do not lead to safety"), E3's output is correct given its input — it is not biased. Correcting
E3's output directly (via cognitive restructuring: "the situation is not as dangerous as you
think") does not repair the input. Only rebuilding E2 through actual action in the world
restores the capacity model.

**Clinical test:** Within avoidance-disorder treatment trials, measure separately the patient's
threat evaluation (E3 proxy: catastrophic appraisal ratings) and capacity estimate (E2 proxy:
self-efficacy for approach, vividly imagined action success rating). Test whether pre-treatment
E2 vs E3 locus of failure predicts differential response to CBT-cognitive vs CBT-behavioural
components in dismantling designs.

**Epistemic status:** Derived, consistent with exposure therapy superiority literature;
E2/E3 dissociation prediction is novel.

### P-010: Goal incoherence (absent E1 schema for goal state) is not responsive to dopaminergic intervention

**Prediction:** A subgroup of patients presenting with anhedonia, motivational flatness, or
absence of goal-directed behaviour will show a failure at the level of goal content (MECH-214:
no E1 schema for the desired state) rather than at the level of wanting signal gain (MECH-187:
dopaminergic incentive salience suppression). This subgroup will not respond to dopaminergic
interventions because the wanting system is intact — there is simply no coherent target for
it to amplify.

**Mechanism:** z_goal (the wanting representation) requires a coherent E1-representable goal
state to point at. If the patient cannot form a stable, affectively grounded representation
of what they want (because E1 has insufficient schema differentiation in the relevant domain),
seeding z_goal is meaningless: the signal is effectively noise with no referent.

**Clinical test:** Assess separately: (1) the capacity to identify and elaborate a desired
state (schema richness proxy: specificity, sensory grounding, affective loading of described
goals), and (2) response to incentive salience probes (wanting scale ratings, motivational
task performance). Predict that low-schema patients show preserved response to immediate
rewards (intact wanting system) but persistent inability to form medium- and long-horizon
goals. These patients should show poor response to antidepressants and better response to
interventions targeting schema enrichment (psychedelic-assisted therapy, arts therapies,
somatic/interoception practices).

**Epistemic status:** Derived, speculative; no direct clinical test identified in the literature.

## How to add a prediction

1. Identify the architectural mechanism that generates the prediction
2. State the prediction precisely enough to be falsifiable
3. Specify the clinical test: what population, what measures, what result would confirm or refute
4. Assign epistemic status
5. Add to the table above and link from the relevant `failure_modes/` entry under `Testable Psychiatric Predictions`

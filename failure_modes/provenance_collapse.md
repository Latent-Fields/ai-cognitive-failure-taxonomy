# Provenance Collapse

## Definition

A system loses track of the source, status, or origin of content in its context — whether
a piece of information was observed, generated, retrieved, recalled, simulated, or inferred.
Without this tracking, generated and retrieved content are treated as equivalent, simulated
outcomes influence decisions as if they were real outcomes, and the system cannot distinguish
what it knows from what it has produced. Outputs may present synthetic content with the
authority of observed fact.

---

## Behavioural Expression in AI

| Context | Observable behaviour |
|---|---|
| RAG systems | Retrieved document content and model-generated elaborations are presented indistinguishably |
| Multi-step reasoning chains | Conclusions from earlier reasoning steps are treated as premises with the same status as input facts |
| Agentic memory | Model-generated diary or memory entries are recalled with the same confidence as user-provided information |
| Simulation-to-decision pipelines | Outcomes of internal rollouts or hypothetical scenarios are used as if they were empirically observed |
| Long-context models | Context contains a mix of user input, prior model outputs, and retrieved text; the model cannot attribute subsequent claims correctly |

---

## Necessary Architectural Motifs

- **Weak comparator** — the system lacks a mechanism that tracks and verifies the source of content
- **Persistent state** — content from multiple sources accumulates in a common store without provenance labels

---

## Systems Likely to Exhibit This

| System class | Why vulnerable |
|---|---|
| RAG pipelines without explicit citation tracking | Retrieved and generated content are concatenated into a shared context; no structural separation |
| Agentic systems with scratchpad reasoning | Reasoning chain mixes premises (from input) and conclusions (from inference) without tagging |
| LLMs with long contexts | Attention mechanisms do not distinguish the source of attended tokens; model treats all context as equivalent |
| Planning agents with internal rollouts | Simulated future outcomes may be processed through the same pipeline as real observations |

---

## Example Expressions

A RAG-based assistant retrieves three documents to answer a factual question. It synthesises
a response drawing on two of the documents and one piece of parametric knowledge. In a follow-up
question, the user asks for the source. The model attributes the parametric claim to one of the
retrieved documents, because the content occupies the same position in its effective context as
retrieved material.

An agentic system with a persistent scratchpad reasons across multiple steps. At step 7, it
uses a claim it generated at step 3 as a premise, treating it as equivalent to the facts
provided in the original task brief. The step-3 claim was a plausible inference, not a verified
fact. The final output is built on an unverified intermediate conclusion presented as established.

A planning agent runs a simulation of a proposed action sequence, predicting good outcomes.
It then commits to the action sequence treating the simulated outcomes as if they were evidence
of prior success. The decision pipeline does not distinguish "this worked in simulation" from
"this worked previously in the real environment."

## Psychopathology Analogue (Structural, Not Experiential)

**Analogue:** Confabulation (provenance variant); PTSD intrusion; source monitoring failure

**Structural correspondence:** Source monitoring failure in cognitive neuropsychology refers to
the inability to correctly attribute the origin of a memory — whether it was perceived,
imagined, heard, or generated. In PTSD, replayed episodes are experienced as occurring in
the present rather than in the past — the temporal and source tag is lost. In both cases, the
structural feature is the same: a failure of the mechanism that tags content with its origin
and status, causing that content to be processed as equivalent to live observation. The AI case
shares this: content from different sources (retrieved, generated, simulated, recalled) is
processed without a source tag, causing the system to treat all content as having equivalent
epistemic status.

**What this analogy does NOT claim:** PTSD intrusions involve re-experiencing with full
phenomenological immediacy — sensory, emotional, and temporal. Source monitoring failures in
amnesia involve a subjective experience of confidence about false origins. Neither experiential
dimension is present or applicable in the AI case. The AI case is a tagging architecture
problem.

## REE Interpretation

The RC loop is responsible for tracking whether content is real input, generated by E1/E2,
simulated via E3 rollouts, or recalled via replay. In REE, the hypothesis tag (MECH-094)
marks internally generated or replayed content so it is not processed through the real-outcome
pipeline. Provenance collapse occurs when this tag is absent or ignored: E3 evaluates simulated
trajectories as if they were real observations; replay episodes re-enter the residue field
update as if they were new real-world consequences. The RC loop's source-tracking function is
the architectural mechanism whose failure produces this mode.

---

## Known Mitigations in AI

- **Explicit source tagging throughout the pipeline**: maintain a provenance field on every
  content item in context, scratchpad, or memory; never allow source-untagged content to enter
  decision-critical paths
- **Separate channels for retrieved vs generated content**: architecturally distinguish the
  pathways for verified external input and model-generated content; do not merge them into a
  shared context without explicit labelling
- **Simulation/hypothesis tagging**: mark any content produced by internal rollout, simulation,
  or hypothetical reasoning as such; prevent it from updating real-outcome stores
- **Citation-forced output formats**: require the model to attribute every factual claim to a
  source token in its context window; claims without attribution are flagged
- **Temporal and source metadata preservation**: in long-context and memory-augmented systems,
  preserve when and how each content item was acquired

---

## Human Analogue Interventions (Optional, analogy only)

Trauma therapies such as EMDR are thought to work in part by helping patients re-tag traumatic
memories as past events rather than present-tense intrusions. The structural analog for AI:
explicitly reprocessing context at each step to ensure that previously generated content is
tagged as generated, not re-admitted as observation.

---

## Limits of Analogy

1. PTSD involves an affective, somatic, and phenomenological re-experiencing that is central
   to its clinical picture. AI provenance collapse is a tagging architecture failure with no
   affective dimension.
2. Source monitoring failure in amnesia involves a disruption to a normally functioning memory
   system. AI provenance collapse often reflects a system that was never designed with source
   tracking; there is no prior intact function being disrupted.
3. Therapeutic approaches for PTSD address neurobiological dysregulation, interpersonal safety,
   and phenomenological processing — none of which has any analog in AI architectural remediation.
